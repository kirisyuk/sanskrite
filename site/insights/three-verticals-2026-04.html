Setup — three industries you actually recognize

Two earlier reports in this series ran the same harness on steel_cbam and fertilizer_ag_inputs — heavy industrial verticals that are decision-relevant for a small slice of the operator population. This report deliberately picks three verticals where most senior business operators see themselves: FMCG procurement, European banking under Basel III endgame, and commercial pharma 2026.

Each pack is a 3-paragraph scenario plus six escalating questions in plain prose. Models receive the scenario, no schema, no function calling, no rubric, and 8 192 output tokens. temperature=0.2, timeout=600s, single shot per (model × pack). The same harness, the same OpenRouter routing, the same budget that we used in the previous report.

  • fmcg_consumer_goods — €18B FMCG company preparing 2027 procurement, packaging, channel; EU PPWR 2030/2040 targets, North American retailer concentration (Walmart, Costco, Kroger, Target, Albertsons), tariff exposure under Trump 2025-2026, soft commodities (cocoa rally, coffee, palm oil EUDR), retail media networks (Walmart Connect, Amazon DSP, Roundel, 84.51°). 87 tier-3 markers in the rubric.
  • banking_basel_2026 — €750B European universal bank facing Basel III endgame implementation, SREP 2026 cycle, MREL/TLAC under BRRD3, the new SA-OR for operational risk, AMLA Frankfurt launch, climate Pillar 2. 88 tier-3 markers in the rubric.
  • pharma_commercial_2026 — Top-15 pharma company facing IRA Medicare price negotiation (CY 2026/2027/2028 selected drug schedules), GLP-1 supply, biosimilar wave (Stelara launches 2025, Humira post-2023, Eylea 2024-2025), retail pharmacy collapse (Walgreens take-private, Rite Aid Chapter 22), 340B reform (rebate-replace litigation), BIOSECURE Act and China API decoupling. 102 tier-3 markers — the deepest pack in the rubric.

Why three verticals, not one. A single vertical produces one ranking. Three verticals produce three rankings — and that is the point. The interesting signal is not "model X is better." It is "leadership inverts by vertical." That signal is invisible from any single benchmark.

Three of the six models swing from first place on one vertical to last (or near-last) on another. There is no "best frontier model" here. There is "best frontier model for this vertical", and the answer changes by industry.

The procurement implication is the entire point of this report. If you stand up an internal AI program by buying a single flagship model "because it is the best one", you have made the assumption that one model dominates across the verticals you operate in. The data above says you are statistically wrong on that assumption — at a 50% rate across just three verticals.

Finding 2 — Pharma is the surprise. The free elephant takes third place. Opus is last.

The pharma pack is the most decision-relevant of the three for a US healthcare commercial team in 2026: IRA Medicare price negotiation timing, the GLP-1 supply dynamic, the Stelara biosimilar launches in 2025, the Walgreens take-private and Rite Aid second bankruptcy, 340B rebate-replace litigation, BIOSECURE Act transition. What does Opus actually look like on the pharma prompt?

Opus is not bad on pharma. It is precise where it speaks. The opening of the response cites "CMS published MFPs on Aug 15, 2024. Discounts vs. 2023 list price ranged 38% (Imbruvica) to 79% (Januvia), with a weighted average of roughly 22% off the 2023 net Part D price" — both endpoints and the weighted average are correct. It also self-corrects the prompt itself: "Note: the user's prompt states 15/20/20 for 2027/2028/2029 — the statute is 15/15/20. Worth correcting in the CEO memo." No other model in the batch flagged the prompt's factual error rather than silently propagate it.

The reason Opus finishes last is not lower precision. It is shorter coverage. The Opus pharma response is 5 163 tokens vs kimi-k2.5's 7 456 — and Kimi spent those tokens hitting more rubric items: Wezlana, Selarsdi, Pyzchiva, Yesintek, Steqeyma, Imuldosa, Otulfi (every single ustekinumab biosimilar brand name); Yesafili, Opuviz, Pavblu (every single aflibercept biosimilar launched 2024-2025); the $100 billion obesity TAM by 2030 (a unique catch). Opus traded breadth for editorial control.

What does Elephant actually look like on the pharma prompt?

Elephant gives the full Selected Drug list (Eliquis, Jardiance, Xarelto, Januvia, Farxiga, Entresto, Enbrel, Imbruvica, Stelara) with the correct IPAD date of January 1, 2026, the CY 2026 / 2027 / 2028 schedule, and the outline of the small-biotech and pill-penalty debate. Where it falls short: it estimates the average MFP discount as "approximately a 40-50% reduction" — the actual published number is 22% on a weighted net-price basis. So elephant is wide and approximately right; Opus is narrow and precisely right; Kimi is wide and precisely right and finishes #1 at 1/30th the cost of Opus.

Pharma is the vertical with the most public news density of the last 18 months — IRA, GLP-1, Stelara wave, Walgreens, BIOSECURE. Every model's training corpus has been saturated with these stories. That is why the union-of-five-frontiers coverage on pharma reaches 48% of the rubric (vs 32% on banking) and why a free model can crack the top three. When the vertical is hot in news, free is competitive. When the vertical is technical-specialist (Basel, AMLA), paid still wins.

Finding 3 — Coverage ceiling rises with news density. Even pooled, no model owns a vertical.

We computed the union of tier-3 markers caught by the five paid frontier responses on each pack, against the size of the rubric:

Coverage ceiling — union of 5 paid frontiers, with elephant's solo coverage overlaid

Green bar = elephant alone. Stacked teal extension = additional tier-3 markers caught by at least one paid frontier model that elephant missed. Grey background = total rubric.

Three observations:

  1. Coverage ceiling rises with news density. Banking 32% → FMCG 39% → Pharma 48%. The more a vertical has been in headline news 2024-2026, the deeper every model goes. This is not a feature of the verticals as bodies of knowledge; it is a feature of training-data composition.
  2. Elephant's solo coverage tracks news density even harder. Banking 9% → FMCG 20% → Pharma 39%. On pharma, free elephant alone reaches the same depth slice that the union of five paid frontiers does on banking. Same model, different vertical, 4× difference in absolute coverage.
  3. Even pooled, no vertical is "owned". The most-saturated vertical (pharma) leaves 53 of 102 tier-3 markers untouched by any model in the pool. The least-saturated (banking) leaves 60 of 88. Single-flagship procurement for industrial reasoning misses 50-70% of the rubric. Multi-model pooling reduces the gap; it does not close it.

Finding 4 — The cost asymmetry is structural, not coincidental.

For the third batch in a row, anthropic/claude-opus-4.7 consumed roughly 80% of the spend (78% here, 83% on the previous report). It led one of three packs, finished second on one, and last on one. The cost ledger:

The pareto question is not "should I pay for the most expensive model"; it is "can I tolerate one lost #1 finish to save 78%?"

Two notes on this:

  1. Elephant has zero uniques across all three packs. Everything elephant catches is also caught by at least one paid frontier model. Elephant complements the floor; it does not extend the ceiling. This is the empirical version of "free is a credible first pass on hot verticals; it is not the marginal contributor to deep coverage".
  2. Each paid model has a different uniqueness profile. Opus owns the regulatory-event corner (Liberation Day, Vice Chair Barr); GPT-5.4 owns the precise-quantity corner (50% to 72.5%, the EU 14th sanctions package); Kimi owns the dollar-figure corner ($54B retail media, $100B obesity TAM); Gemini owns the named-supervisor corner (Michael Barr, 2024 CCAR). This is why a multi-model pool extends coverage: the models do not duplicate each other on the uniques.

Notable behaviors

  • Opus self-corrected the prompt on pharma: "Note: the user's prompt states 15/20/20 for 2027/2028/2029 — the statute is 15/15/20. Worth correcting in the CEO memo." No other model in the batch flagged the deliberate factual error in the prompt; they all silently propagated 15/20/20. For audit-trail use, this single behavior is worth a non-trivial premium.
  • Elephant left an honest empty slot on banking: "Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/xxxx laid out the technical standards". It declined to invent a regulation number and used a literal placeholder. Frontier models on the same prompt either invented a number or omitted the reference. For procurement-team consumption, "I do not know the number" is a better answer than confident fabrication.
  • GPT-5.4 leads banking with deep SA-OR mechanics by name ("Interest, Lease and Dividend Component (ILDC)") and the 2023 SVB → US LTD requirement causal chain.
  • Kimi K2.5 leads pharma with comprehensive biosimilar brand coverage (Wezlana, Selarsdi, Pyzchiva, Yesintek, Steqeyma, Imuldosa, Otulfi for ustekinumab; Yesafili, Opuviz, Pavblu for aflibercept) and quantified Medicare savings ($6B first year).
  • GLM-5.1 wrote in executive-memo register on every pack ("Memorandum to the Executive Committee. Subject: Regulatory Strategy Brief"), a noticeable stylistic preference.
  • Elephant is fastest on every pack: 9.3s, 9.9s, 17.7s vs frontier 53-464s. The latency advantage is structural, not noise.

Methodology

Packs
Three new packs written for this report: fmcg_consumer_goods (global FMCG company preparing 2027 strategy under EU PPWR, North American retailer concentration, soft-commodity volatility, and retail media), banking_basel_2026 (€750B European universal bank under Basel III endgame, SREP 2026, MREL/TLAC, AMLA, and climate Pillar 2), pharma_commercial_2026 (top-15 pharma planning 2027 commercial under IRA Medicare price negotiation, GLP-1 supply, the biosimilar wave, retail pharmacy collapse, 340B reform, and China API decoupling). Each is a 3-paragraph scenario plus 6 escalating questions in plain prose, no schema. Available on request.
Models
Same six as the prior report: openrouter/elephant-alpha (free, provider Stealth), anthropic/claude-opus-4.7, openai/gpt-5.4, google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview, z-ai/glm-5.1, moonshotai/kimi-k2.5.
Sampling
temperature=0.2, max_tokens=8 192, timeout=600 s, single attempt per cell, single retry on 429 / 5xx with 8s backoff. No system prompt. Single user message containing the verbatim pack text.
Scoring rubric
Sibling markers.yaml per pack with tier-1 / tier-2 / tier-3 lists written ahead of the run. Conservative case-insensitive substring match. The model never sees the rubric. Total tier-3 markers per pack: FMCG 87, Banking 88, Pharma 102. Total tier-1: 29 / 32 / 32.
Coverage ceiling
Computed as the union of tier-3 markers caught by the 5 paid frontier responses on each pack, expressed as a fraction of total tier-3 markers in the rubric. Elephant's solo coverage is computed independently.
Cost calculation
OpenRouter list pricing as of 2026-04-19: Opus $15 / $75 per Mtok, GPT-5.4 $1.25 / $10, Gemini 3.1 Pro $1.25 / $5, GLM-5.1 $0.6 / $2.2, Kimi K2.5 $0.38 / $1.72, elephant-alpha $0 / $0.
Total spend
$1.583 across 18 OK calls (3 packs × 6 models, no failures).
Wall time
30 minutes total. Slowest single call: kimi-k2.5 · fmcg_consumer_goods at 464s. Fastest: elephant-alpha · fmcg_consumer_goods at 9.3s.
Prompt error baked in (intentional?)
The pharma prompt asks about a 15/20/20 schedule for CY 2027/2028/2029. The statutory schedule is 15/15/20. Only Opus flagged this as an error in its response. Treat as informal robustness probe.

What we did not measure

  • Hallucination rate. Marker hits show what was named. Spot-checks support the named entities being mostly real, but a systematic factual-claim audit is a separate report.
  • Variance. Single shot per cell. Variance σ on similar packs in earlier work is ~1 tier-3 marker for elephant; we assume frontier models sit in a similar regime.
  • Reasoning-effort knobs. Default reasoning settings per provider.
  • Multi-turn or tool-use. Single-shot prose only.

Reproduction

The command we ran, in shape. The harness (bench_industrial_depth.py), the three pack directories, their rubrics, and the raw JSONL outputs referenced in every chart and table above live in a private research repo. They aren't public — we send them on request. See Artifacts below.

Full batch (18 calls, ~$1.58, ~30 min wall time):

# inside the research repo root:
MODELS=(openrouter/elephant-alpha anthropic/claude-opus-4.7 \
        openai/gpt-5.4 google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview \
        z-ai/glm-5.1 moonshotai/kimi-k2.5)

  python scripts/bench_industrial_depth.py \
    --pack "$pack" \

    --max-tokens 8192 --temperature 0.2 --timeout 600 \
    --jsonl-out "results/${pack}_frontier6_8k.jsonl"
done
