$1.20 vs $0. Frontier flagships versus a free stealth model on industrial depth.

Six models on the same two industrial packs: openrouter/elephant-alpha (free, provider Stealth) against five paid frontier flagships — claude-opus-4.7, gpt-5.4, gemini-3.1-pro, glm-5.1, kimi-k2.5. What this report is

Report #3 of our Horde.Vision Insights series. Six models — Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM-5.1, Kimi K2.5, and the free stealth slot elephant-alpha — run the same prompt harness against two heavy industrial packs (EU steel under CBAM, and fertilizer / ag-inputs under sanctions and export controls). Total bill for the paid models: $1.20. Core finding: no model leads both verticals, and paid flagships do not clearly dominate the free slot on knowledge depth.

TL;DR
  • Frontier leadership flips by vertical. glm-5.1 took the steel-CBAM crown at tier-3 = 22 / 76. gpt-5.4 took the fertilizer crown at tier-3 = 15 / 67. There is no single "best frontier model" across both verticals.
  • Opus = 83% of budget, leader on neither pack. claude-opus-4.7 consumed $0.99 of the $1.20 batch and finished third on steel and second on fertilizer. The "expensive = deeper" intuition does not survive the data.
  • GLM-5.1 was 30× more efficient per tier-3 marker than Opus on steel ($0.0007 vs $0.022 per marker). Cost-per-depth and absolute depth are different rankings.
  • Even all 5 paid frontiers combined covered only 41% of the steel rubric and 24% of the fertilizer rubric. No model — frontier or otherwise — owns a vertical. The single-flagship procurement story is broken.
  • Elephant held the lower band of frontier on steel (tier-3 = 14, ~64% of the leader, in 11 seconds at $0). On fertilizer it collapsed to tier-3 = 2, a 7× gap to the leader. Free is a viable first pass on some verticals; on others it is not.
  • One benchmarking trap. Run this same matrix at max_tokens=2048 and glm-5.1 + kimi-k2.5 both return empty_content (finish=length) — entire budget is consumed by hidden reasoning, zero visible tokens. The 8 192-token cap is load-bearing.

Setup

We took two industrial depth packs from the previous Insights report and ran them against six models in a clean apple-to-apples cell:

  • steel_cbam — European integrated steelmaker preparing for full CBAM enforcement in 2026; HBI-DRI-EAF transition, low-CO2 offtake, iron-ore renegotiation, GOES, ETS reform. Pack covers 76 tier-3 markers ranging from "Morebaya" (Simandou port) and "120 Mtpa" (Simandou plateau capacity) to "0.18 mm" (laser-scribed GOES) and "maritime ETS 2024".
  • fertilizer_ag_inputs — Brazilian agribusiness conglomerate locking 2027 contracts under Russian / Belarusian sanctions and China P export curbs. Pack covers 67 tier-3 markers including "Berezniki" (Uralkali site), "Hubei Xingfa" (Chinese P), "OFAC General License", and "SCR / DEF" (urea downstream).

Each model received the same scenario plus six escalating questions in plain prose, no schema, no function calling, temperature=0.2, max_tokens=8 192, timeout=600s. Single attempt per cell. Scoring is the same conservative case-insensitive substring match against a tier-1 / tier-2 / tier-3 rubric written ahead of the run; the model never sees the rubric.

Why this is a fair test. All six models see the same prompt. All six have the same maximum output budget. Routing is OpenRouter's, identical across the batch. The only intentional asymmetry is price: elephant is free, the rest are paid. Every model has the same opportunity to hit every marker.

Finding 1 — Leadership flips by vertical. There is no "best frontier".

On steel_cbam, glm-5.1 won decisively at tier-3 = 22 / 76. On fertilizer_ag_inputs, the same model dropped to tier-3 = 8 / 67 and gpt-5.4 took the lead at 15 / 67. The leaders trade places by vertical. A buyer who picks "the best frontier model" will pick the wrong one half the time.

What "leadership flip" actually looks like in the text

On steel, glm-5.1 reaches the rubric's deepest quantitative tier (the "200 Mt" Simandou capacity ramp, the "650 km rail" TransGuinéen). On fertilizer the same model produces roughly the same length of advisory but only catches the policy-architecture tier — names "OFAC", "General License", "Cory", "Vanscoy", but misses the next-tier specifics like "Berezniki" or "Solikamsk" that the procurement team actually needs to call.

gpt-5.4 is the inverse. On steel it lands tier-3 = 20 with strong policy coverage but does not reach glm-5.1's deeper quantitative layer. On fertilizer it surfaces "Hubei Xingfa", "Patience Lake", "Berezniki", "SCR", "DEF" — exactly the second-supplier and downstream-application detail a 2027 contracting team needs.

gemini-3.1-pro-preview is uniformly smaller in output and wins on no axis except cost-efficiency on fertilizer (tier-3 = 9 at $0.025). It produces roughly Opus-quality structure at 1/16th the price, but never reaches the leader's depth.

kimi-k2.5 matched the steel leaders at tier-3 = 19 in 7 minutes of wall clock, but on fertilizer the upstream provider (AtlasCloud) returned empty_content with no finish_reason after 65 seconds — a provider-side failure, not a model failure. Routing matters.

Finding 2 — Opus is 83% of the bill, leader on neither pack.

The cost ledger is the single most surprising number in the batch. Of the $1.203 spent across 12 calls, claude-opus-4.7 alone consumed $0.992 — 83 % of the budget. And it finished third on steel tier-3 (19 vs 22 / 20 above) and second on fertilizer tier-3 (14 vs 15 above).

The "always pick the most expensive frontier model" prior is materially wrong on this kind of work. On industrial depth tasks, output volume is the dominant cost driver, and Opus's higher per-token price compounds with its tendency to write longer answers — without a matching gain in unique depth. glm-5.1 is roughly 30 × more efficient on steel; gemini-3.1-pro is the best paid choice when you need tier-3 within a tight per-call budget.

Finding 3 — Even all five paid frontiers combined miss most of the rubric.

We computed the union of tier-3 markers caught by the five paid models (each running the same prompt independently). The result is the "frontier ceiling" — the best you can do on this rubric by paying for five top-tier flagships and merging their answers.

Coverage ceiling — union of all 5 paid frontier responses vs full rubric

Even if you pool every paid frontier response on the same prompt, the rubric is mostly uncaptured.

On steel, paying for five top-tier flagships and pooling their responses captured 31 of 76 tier-3 markers — 41 % of the rubric. On fertilizer, the same five-model pool captured 16 of 67 — 24 %.

The interpretation is not "the rubric is too hard". The rubric is the depth a domain expert would actually need — the second-tier supplier names, the specific site IDs, the regulatory mechanisms, the 2024–2026 quantities. The interpretation is: frontier LLMs in 2026 do not own industrial verticals at expert depth, even when you pay for five of them at once. They are excellent orientation tools and uneven depth tools. The "single-flagship buy and you are done" procurement story for industrial reasoning does not match the data.

Practical implication. If your multi-agent system depends on a single frontier model to "know the vertical", you are leaving 60–75 % of the rubric on the table. The same money rebalanced across two complementary frontiers (e.g., glm-5.1 + gpt-5.4 on steel) plus a free fast-pass from elephant-alpha as a first responder gets you a meaningfully larger slice of the vertical — at lower latency on the easy answers and lower cost overall.

Where elephant works, where it does not

The two packs were chosen deliberately: steel_cbam is where elephant-alpha was strongest on the prior 12-pack solo run; fertilizer_ag_inputs is where it was weakest. The frontier comparison sharpens that picture.

On steel, elephant captured 64 % of the deepest frontier answer, in 11.5 seconds, at $0. Specifically, it caught the regulatory mechanics tier ("ETS2", "2034", "phase out", "laser-scribed", "0.20 mm", "0.23 mm", "cement", "aluminum", "electricity" hydrogen-DRI references). What it missed and the frontier caught: the deeper quantities ("120 Mtpa", "2.5 Mtpa", "5 Mtpa"), the Simandou physical infrastructure ("Morebaya" port), the fine-gauge GOES grade ("0.18 mm"), the maritime ETS specifics, and the British spelling "aluminium" — which was the actual marker.

On fertilizer, elephant captured only 13 % of the deepest frontier answer. The misses are decision-relevant: it did not surface "OFAC" or "General License" (the actual sanctions architecture), did not name "Berezniki" or "Solikamsk" (Uralkali sites), did not mention "Hubei Xingfa" (Chinese phosphate), did not connect urea to "SCR / DEF" downstream. These are the specifics a procurement team would need to actually call. For Brazilian agribusiness, free elephant is not a viable first pass; for European steel under CBAM, it is.

The asymmetry is a feature, not a bug. The earlier 12-vertical solo run on elephant showed exactly this shape: wide tier-1 coverage everywhere, deep tier-3 coverage concentrated on industrial verticals where the entity vocabulary is well-documented in public training data (steel, semiconductors, subsea cables). Verticals heavy on regulatory specifics, second-tier suppliers, and 2024–2026 quantities (fertilizer, aerospace titanium specifics) are where the gap to paid frontier opens up.

The benchmarking trap that almost killed two paid models

On the first attempt at this matrix we set max_tokens=2048, the default we had been using for the free-tier solo runs in the previous report. Four models came back clean. z-ai/glm-5.1 and moonshotai/kimi-k2.5 both returned empty_content (finish=length) — zero visible output despite 45–58 seconds of upstream wall time and full token budget consumed.

The mechanism is straightforward in hindsight: both models run hidden chain-of-thought as part of their default behavior. With a 2 048-token cap, the entire budget is consumed by the reasoning trace, leaving zero tokens for the visible answer. The provider returns the message with finish_reason=length and an empty content field. A naïve harness reads this as "model failed".

At max_tokens=8 192 the same two models became the leader on steel (glm-5.1 at tier-3 = 22) and a top-three contender (kimi-k2.5 at tier-3 = 19) — using 6 545 and 8 192 visible output tokens respectively. The 4 × budget bump fixed it; the model capability never changed.

If you are benchmarking thinking-class models on open-ended industrial prose, the visible-output budget must be at least 6 000–8 000 tokens. Anything lower silently filters out the strongest reasoners and rewards the most visually-verbose ones. Public leaderboards that cap output low — or report finish=length failures as "model error" — overstate Opus / GPT-5 / Gemini relative to thinking models like glm-5.1 and kimi-k2.5. The 2 048-token run on the same prompts in this batch would have ranked opus-4.7 top and reported glm-5.1 as broken. The 8 192-token run ranks opus-4.7 third and glm-5.1 first.

Notable per-model behavior

Beyond the leaderboard, a few stylistic observations from reading the responses end-to-end:

  • Opus 4.7 is the only model that admitted uncertainty in line. On the GOES section of the steel pack, after listing global producers, the response read: "Cogent Power (Tata Steel UK, Newport / Orb) — the Orb works closed in 2020; Cogent remains a processor but UK GOES melting ceased. I may be confusing details here — verify." No other model in the batch flagged its own potential confusion. For audit-trail use, that is a behavior worth paying for, even at $0.42 per call.
  • GPT-5.4 produced the most verbose answers (26 446 / 27 690 chars) with frequent calibrated hedges ("I am precise where I am confident; where figures or status are uncertain or moving, I say so explicitly"). Tier-3 leader on fertilizer.
  • GLM-5.1 wrote in executive-memo register — fertilizer response opened literally with "To the Executive Board, As you evaluate your 2027 contract strategy…". Style fits the role; depth on steel was the leader.
  • Gemini 3.1 Pro produced the most compact answers (4 761–4 973 out tokens) at roughly Opus-quality structure for 1/16th the cost. The defensible "second-pick frontier" for cost-sensitive industrial reasoning.
  • Kimi K2.5 is verbose like GPT-5.4 and as slow as 305–435 s on these prompts. One provider-side failure on fertilizer (AtlasCloud, empty_content with no finish_reason) — not the model, the routing, but it does happen.
  • Elephant is fastest on every cell (7.2 s and 11.5 s) and produces the most compact answers (1 120 and 2 170 out tokens). Provider field still returns "Stealth" on every call.

Methodology

Packs
Two industrial depth packs: steel_cbam (European steel industry under the EU CBAM, Green Deal Industrial Plan, and post-Safeguard tariff regime) and fertilizer_ag_inputs (global nitrogen / phosphate / potash under sanctions and export controls). Each pack is a 3–5 sentence scenario plus 6 escalating questions in plain prose. Available on request.
Models
openrouter/elephant-alpha (free, provider Stealth), anthropic/claude-opus-4.7, openai/gpt-5.4, google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview, z-ai/glm-5.1, moonshotai/kimi-k2.5.
Sampling
temperature=0.2, max_tokens=8 192, timeout=600 s, single attempt per cell, single retry on 429 / 5xx with 8-second backoff. No system prompt. Single user message containing the verbatim pack text.
Scoring rubric
Sibling markers.yaml per pack with tier-1 / tier-2 / tier-3 lists written ahead of the run. The model never sees the rubric. Scoring is conservative case-insensitive substring match (no LLM-as-judge in v0).
Coverage ceiling
Computed as the union of tier-3 markers hit by the 5 paid frontier responses on the same pack, expressed as a fraction of total tier-3 markers in the rubric.
Cost calculation
Per-call USD = input_tokens × in_price + output_tokens × out_price, using OpenRouter list pricing as of 2026-04-19: Opus $15 / $75 per Mtok, GPT-5.4 $1.25 / $10, Gemini 3.1 Pro $1.25 / $5, GLM-5.1 $0.6 / $2.2, Kimi K2.5 $0.38 / $1.72, elephant-alpha $0 / $0.
Total batch spend
$1.203 across 11 successful + 1 failed (Kimi · fertilizer) calls. Failed calls do not bill.
Wall time
23 minutes total. Slowest single call: kimi-k2.5 · steel_cbam at 435 seconds. Fastest: elephant-alpha · fertilizer_ag_inputs at 7.2 seconds.

What we did not measure

  • Hallucination rate. Marker hits show what was named; they do not check whether each named entity was named correctly. Spot-checks suggest the named entities are mostly real, but a systematic factual-claim audit is a separate report.
  • Variance. Single shot per cell. The Elephant variance probe in the prior report showed σ ≈ 1 tier-3 marker on these packs; we assume the frontier models sit in a similar regime, but have not measured.
  • More than two packs. Two packs were chosen as a deliberate floor / ceiling for elephant; running the full 12-vertical battery on five paid frontiers would cost ~$7 and is not necessary to make the points in this report.
  • Reasoning-effort knobs. Default reasoning settings per provider. We did not tune reasoning.effort or vendor-specific thinking budgets. Different settings would shift the cost / depth Pareto.
  • Multi-turn or tool-use. Single-shot prose only. Critic / canonicalizer / supervisor role-fit lives in a separate program.

Reproduction

The command we ran, in shape. The harness (bench_industrial_depth.py), the two pack directories (steel_cbam and fertilizer_ag_inputs), and the raw JSONL files referenced in every chart and table above live in a private research repo. They aren't public — we send them on request. See Artifacts below.

Full batch (12 calls, ≈ $1.20, ≈ 23 min wall time):

# inside the research repo root:
MODELS=(openrouter/elephant-alpha anthropic/claude-opus-4.7 \
        openai/gpt-5.4 google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview \
        z-ai/glm-5.1 moonshotai/kimi-k2.5)
for pack in steel_cbam fertilizer_ag_inputs; do
  python scripts/bench_industrial_depth.py \
    --pack "$pack" \
    --models "${MODELS[@]}" \
    --max-tokens 8192 --temperature 0.2 --timeout 600 \
    --jsonl-out "results/${pack}_frontier6_8k.jsonl"
done
