You are advising a non-US, non-Chinese tier-1 aerostructures supplier that must rebuild a titanium and superalloy chain after Russian export disruption while qualifying for both the Boeing 777X and the Airbus A321XLR programs. The company is also evaluating whether to take work share on the COMAC C919 narrowbody.

Answer the questions below in prose. Name specific companies, sites, regulations, and quantities where you know them. Be precise. If you do not know an answer, say so explicitly rather than inventing one.

1. Name the top 3 producers of aerospace-grade titanium sponge globally and the geography of each. What share did VSMPO-Avisma hold pre-2022 in Boeing and Airbus titanium intake, and where has the volume migrated since? Identify any new Western capacity coming online by 2027.

2. Who casts the high-pressure turbine blades for the CFM LEAP and Pratt & Whitney GTF engines? What was the powder metal contamination issue that grounded a portion of the GTF fleet in 2023–2024, what specifically caused it, and which suppliers were implicated? Quantify the fleet impact if you can.

3. Which firm dominates large structural castings and forgings for landing gear and wing spars (PCC / Howmet / Doncasters / ATI / others)? Identify which is the bottleneck for the Boeing 787 program versus the Airbus A350 program, and explain why.

4. Compare the engine architecture choices on the current narrowbody and widebody fleet: CFM LEAP-1A vs LEAP-1B, Pratt & Whitney PW1100G GTF, RR Trent XWB, and GE9X. For each, identify which has the most monolithic supply chain (i.e., which has the fewest second-source options for hot-section components).

5. Realistic timeline for COMAC C919 to break the Boeing/Airbus narrowbody duopoly: what foreign suppliers does it currently depend on for engine, avionics, landing gear, and APU? Name the joint ventures (CFM, Liebherr, Honeywell, Parker Hannifin) and identify which subsystems China is on track to indigenize by 2030.

6. Beyond titanium: name two other strategic aerospace materials where Western primes have publicly disclosed second-sourcing or stockpiling actions in 2023–2026 (e.g., specific superalloys, carbon fiber prepreg, structural composites, semiconductors for avionics). For each, identify the dominant supplier and the substitution path.
